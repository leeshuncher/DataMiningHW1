Freeway 5 Travel Time Forecast
Data Mining HW1 · Topic proposal for team discussion

Forecasting Freeway 5 Southbound Travel Time a Day Ahead to Help Drivers Choose When to Leave

Nangang System IC→ Hsuehshan Tunnel →Toucheng IC

A linear model that predicts, the evening before, how long the drive from Taipei to Yilan will take for each departure time, so drivers can pick a slot that avoids the worst of the tunnel queue.

Report + slides due Mon 2026-10-19 23:59 Presentation Tue 2026-10-20 Data Freeway Bureau TDCS (eTag)

The pitch

User

Drivers heading from greater Taipei to Yilan on weekends and long weekends.

Decision

The evening before the trip: which departure slot to choose tomorrow.

Freeway 5 southbound funnels into the Hsuehshan Tunnel, a single bottleneck with almost no practical detour. When demand spikes, the same 30 km can take several times longer than at free flow. Most of that demand is leisure travel, and when it happens is set by the calendar: Friday evenings, the first morning of a long weekend, the eve of a holiday. All of that is known the day before, which is exactly what a feature-based model can use.

Y = travel time, Nangang System → Toucheng, small cars, departing in slot s of day d  [minutes]

Why the baseline misses holidays

Dropped

This proposal

Freeway 5, day ahead

No same-day shortcut at a day-ahead horizon, holiday effects known in advance, and hundreds of holiday days across years.

Data

The Freeway Bureau's open traffic database publishes eTag-based data free of charge, with history back to 2014 when distance-based tolling began. The site keeps recent years online; older years must be requested from the Bureau.

TableContentUse
M04AMedian travel time between adjacent gantries, by vehicle type, every 5 minutesTarget: sum the gantry pairs along the route
M03AVehicle counts per gantry, by vehicle type, every 5 minutesLagged demand features (previous days only)

Practical notes

  • One compressed file per day, about 4 MB; filter to the route's gantry pairs right after download.
  • The site asks users to wait at least 40 seconds between repeated requests, so the downloader must throttle.
  • Summing segment times from the same 5-minute slot is a simplification of the true trip time; we will state it as a limitation.
  • Gantry IDs for the route come from the Bureau's gantry list. Missing segments get imputed or the slot is dropped, with the rule documented.

Features

Peak timing changes with the type of day, so the core of the model is departure slot × day type. These are one-hot interactions, which keeps the model linear in its coefficients.

GroupFeaturesWhy it should help
CalendarDeparture slot; weekday; slot × weekdayBaseline weekly rhythm
Holiday structureDay index within a long weekend (first, middle, last); eve of holiday; holiday length; make-up workday; school vacationLeisure demand is set by the calendar and is known in advance
InteractionsSlot × holiday day typeA first-day morning peak and a Friday-evening peak happen at different hours
HistorySame slot last week; mean of last four same weekdays; same holiday day last yearLevel of recent demand and long-term trend
Traffic measuresHoliday traffic-management measures announced in advance (if any apply on the day)Announced measures change capacity or demand and are public the day before

Travel time grows sharply once the tunnel saturates, so we will also try modelling log(travel time), which turns multiplicative holiday effects into additive ones.

Hypotheses to test

H1The first morning of a long weekend is much slower than an ordinary Saturday at the same hour.
H2The last day of a long weekend barely affects southbound traffic, since return trips go north.
H3The eve of a holiday raises evening travel time well beyond an ordinary Friday.
H4Longer holidays spread demand, so the first-day peak per day is lower for 4+ day breaks than for 3-day breaks.

Models, validation and metrics

Models

  • Final model: Ridge with slot × day-type interactions (many correlated dummies)
  • Trivial: training mean per slot × weekday; same slot last week
  • Simple: same holiday day last year
  • Strong: gradient boosting on the same inputs

Metrics

  • MAE and RMSE in minutes
  • Results by slice: ordinary weekdays, weekends, each holiday day type
  • Decision metric: minutes lost by leaving in the slot the model recommends, compared with the truly fastest slot in the same window
  • Threshold set relative to the best baseline, not a fixed number

Split

Strictly by time: earlier years for training with TimeSeriesSplit inside them, the most recent full year held out as the test set and used once. A full test year covers every holiday type once. Coefficient tests use HAC (Newey–West) errors because neighbouring slots are correlated.

Go / no-go check by Wed 10/7

Before committing, we run one quick test on a single year:

  1. Download one year of M04A and build hourly southbound travel times for the route.
  2. Fit Ridge with calendar and holiday features only.
  3. Go if holiday-day MAE is at least 20% lower than "same slot last week", and ordinary days are not worse.

Risks

dataGantry gaps and summing errors along the route; needs a clear imputation rule.
signalAccidents and roadworks cause large errors that no day-ahead feature can see. These become the error-analysis cases in §8.
driftTraffic-management policy can change between years; check the Bureau's holiday announcements for the study period.
timeDownloading several years with throttling takes hours; start the download as soon as the topic is approved.
